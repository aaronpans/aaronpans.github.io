Decision Guide 03 · Governance Loop

Inspection vs Closed-Loop Governance

Direct answer: inspection identifies and records a deviation. Closed-loop governance carries the issue through ownership, correction, field evidence, verification, closure, recurrence analysis and prevention.

Decision ruleUse inspection to detect. Use closed-loop governance whenever the deviation is material, recurring or risk-bearing.

Side-by-side comparison

DimensionInspectionClosed-loop governance
Primary purposeCompare current conditions with a standard.Remove the gap, verify the result and prevent recurrence.
End pointA finding, score or report.Verified closure plus learning or prevention when required.
OwnershipInspector or audit function identifies.Issue owner corrects; verifier accepts; management escalates when needed.
EvidenceObservation, photo, record or checklist.Original finding, corrective evidence, verification and closure decision.
Typical failureRepeated findings without durable correction.Administrative closure without field proof or recurrence analysis.
Learning triggerMay reveal a pattern.Pattern changes training, tools, responsibility, controls or standard versions.

The minimum closure chain

  1. Record the deviation and its risk level.
  2. Assign one accountable owner and a deadline.
  3. Complete corrective action and capture field evidence.
  4. Verify against an explicit acceptance criterion.
  5. Close, reject or escalate the issue.
  6. For recurrence, analyze the cause and add preventive action.

Signals that the loop is broken

The same issue appears in consecutive inspections, stores close items with text-only replies, no one can name the verifier, deadlines have no escalation consequence, or reports show completion while field conditions remain unchanged.

Management evidence to review

Review closure aging, overdue critical items, rejected closure rate, recurrence by category, cross-store patterns, preventive-action completion and the standards or training versions changed as a result.

BoundaryThis guide describes a management loop, not a regulatory audit standard. Required evidence and escalation severity should match the organization's risk context and applicable rules.
