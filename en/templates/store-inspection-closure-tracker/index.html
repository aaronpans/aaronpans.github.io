Notes

Eight-stage workflow

  1. Detect and classify: Deviation and provisional risk level recorded.
  2. Capture evidence: Location, time, standard reference and field proof attached.
  3. Assign owner and deadline: One accountable owner accepts an organization-defined deadline.
  4. Contain immediate risk: Unsafe or material exposure is controlled before normal operation continues.
  5. Correct the deviation: Corrective action completed with evidence.
  6. Verify effectiveness: Assigned verifier confirms the acceptance criterion in the field.
  7. Close, reject or escalate: Decision and rationale recorded.
  8. Analyze recurrence and prevent: Recurring or systemic issues trigger root-cause and system action.

Decision rules

  1. Use one record per issue. Name the action performer, accountable owner, assigned verifier and closure approver; record evidence references without exposing personal or confidential information.
  2. Keep the issue open until the agreed acceptance criterion is met and the assigned verifier records field evidence. A correction photo alone does not establish effectiveness.
  3. Record verification result as pass, fail or pending. Record closure decision as open, closed, rejected, escalated or reopened. Close only after a pass, evidence and dated approval; failed or missing verification cannot be closed.
  4. The verifier should not verify their own corrective action. If staffing prevents separation, obtain an independent authorized review and document the exception before closure.
  5. For repeated findings, link the original issue, reopen it or create a linked issue under local policy, and assign root cause, preventive action, owner, due date and effectiveness review. A closed correction does not mean prevention is complete.

Example: correction is not yet closure

Illustrative scenario, not a client case: a shift handover log is missing an owner. Uploading a revised log records correction. The verifier must still check the next handover against the agreed criterion; until that check passes and the approver signs, the record remains open.
